# Dragon Games Sell Time, RaidLords Rations Earnings

Dragon-breeding games run on a chain of interlocking clocks (habitats earn gold up to a storage cap, farms turn gold into food, food levels dragons past a cheap breeding gate, breeding draws a rarity-weighted child from an element-pair pool, and slot-limited hatcheries plus gem-priced islands throttle how fast the collection grows), and the genre earns its money by selling relief from whichever clock a player feels most. Dragon City, the reference title, layers simple 3v3 type-multiplier combat, seven stacked per-dragon upgrade tracks, a weekly trophy ladder, five or six overlapping events and a VIP cycle that demotes players who stop spending on top of that loop, and it still rates 4.8 stars from about 1.5 million ratings even as players call its races pay-to-win. RaidLords, which opened on Robinhood Chain on October 26, 2026, tackles the play-and-earn half of the problem by keeping the whole game in a Postgres backend and putting only money on chain: a $1 ETH entry fee, a fixed-supply 1-billion RAID token from a meme launchpad, deposits into a vault, server-signed withdrawal vouchers and burns. It limits what players can take out with a 50% burn on all spending, per-account sell limits tied to deposits, and mass bans (7,779 accounts in its first days, by the team's own count). For Wyrmhaven, the draft spec's architecture is sound (server-side rules and randomness, season rewards funded by revenue and claimed by Merkle proof, no token), but its game design is not: as written the loop cannot start, the breeding rule makes rare dragons worse parents than commons, habitat caps reverse the genre's pacing of return visits, combat is an underspecified stat check, and the reward pool has no source of money within MVP scope. The decisions that matter most are where the pool's money comes from, who is allowed to earn, and how far tradable dragon NFTs can reach into ranked play.

*Evidence note: the Dragon City fan wiki refused automated access, so Dragon City facts come from the developer's official Help Center and news site, the App Store listing, fan databases derived from game config, and search-result summaries of wiki pages; several figures date from 2013–2015 and are flagged as older. RaidLords' player and burn statistics are self-reported by the team and relayed by one outlet, the researchers identified its vault contract from on-chain evidence rather than anything the team published, and both block explorers blocked automated reads.*

## Six clocks and one premium shortcut define the genre's core loop

Dragon City launched on Facebook in May 2012 and on mobile in 2013 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_City)), and its loop is the clearest template for the genre. Dragons earn **gold** into habitats. Farms turn gold into **food**, food levels dragons, levelled dragons unlock breeding, breeding produces eggs, eggs need hatchery slots, and hatched dragons need habitat space before they can earn more gold. Each system consumes what the previous one produces and limits the next one. Gold is useless without food and housing, food is useless without breeding and battle goals, and new dragons are useless without space. That interlock lets one premium currency (gems) relieve pressure wherever the player feels it, while the free path stays complete but slow.

### Breeding is a lottery paid for with time

Breeding works in two steps. The game takes one element from each parent, then draws the offspring from the pool of dragons that carry that element pair, so parents with more elements produce more possible pairs and larger pools ([Dragon City Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4220-how-does-breeding-work/)). Four inputs move the odds within a pool: the parents' elements, the parents' rarity, a paid per-dragon "empower" level, and an account-wide boost from a special building. The official FAQ says dragon level does not affect the odds ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4222-what-affects-my-breeding-outcome/)). Rarer or empowered parents raise the chance of the pool's **top two rarity tiers**, but the interface shows this only as a green bar, never as a percentage ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4221-how-can-i-boost-my-breeding-chances/)). A deterministic layer sits on top of the random one. Specific parent pairs unlock a unique offspring that ordinary breeding cannot produce, at a fixed chance that empower does not change, and some pairs require both parents at a set level such as 30. The pairing screen lists every possible result with its rarity and whether the player already owns it ([official news](https://www.dragoncitygame.com/news/breeding-update); [Ditlep calculator](https://www.ditlep.com/breeding/calculator)). Sources disagree on when this overhaul shipped. The official news post is dated December 2024, while the App Store notes list an update that made more than 200 additional dragons breedable in a September 2026 version ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dragon-city-collect-battle/id561941526)).

The game spans **seven rarity tiers and 22 elements, and a dragon can have up to four elements**. Both parents must be at least **level 4** to breed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_City)). Fan tools built from game config model results as rarity "buckets". When a bucket has no candidates, the remaining weights are rescaled to sum to 100%, and access is gated by parent level and by a "breeding power" derived from rarity ([Ditlep](https://www.ditlep.com/breeding/calculator)). Older community data from 2014, which is unofficial, adds three details. The chance of a specific legendary was about **1% per attempt**. Breeding time depended on the *result's* rarity, so a long timer told the player they had hit something rare. Opposing elements could not breed directly ([Gamelytic](https://gamelytic.com/dragon-city-complete-dragon-breeding-guide/)). Taken together, this is a gacha paid for in time rather than money. Multi-element parents act as keys to bigger pools, and designers can add a species by inserting it into an existing pool instead of writing a new recipe. Empower works as a monetized, persistent odds modifier: a pity mechanism sold one dragon at a time.

### Hatcheries and habitats turn waiting into appointments

Players start with one breeding site and one hatchery. Upgrading the hatchery so more than one egg incubates at once is a standard early recommendation ([BlueStacks](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/dragon-city/drc-tips-tricks-en.html)). Old snippets price those upgrades at about **15, 30 and 60 gems for two, three and four eggs** ([SuperCheats, search snippet](https://www.supercheats.com/guides/dragon-city/hatching)). That is cheap enough for a starter gem grant to make the first premium spend feel effortless. Common dragons breed in **30 seconds and hatch in 30 seconds** ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/dragon/Flame)), so the whole first cycle fits inside the tutorial. Longer timers that scale with rarity appear only after the core actions are learned, and gems or rewarded ads skip them ([BlueStacks](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/dragon-city/drc-tips-tricks-en.html)).

Habitats are element-locked: a dragon must share the habitat's element, apart from a few special habitats that accept any dragon ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4226-what-are-the-different-types-of-habitats/)). Each additional habitat of the same element costs more, and elements unlocked later cost more ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/1212-how-do-habitat-prices-work/)). The number of habitats a player may build rises with player level ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/632-how-many-habitats-can-i-build/)). Upgrades now cost element-specific tokens, largely earned in PvP, or gems, rather than gold alone ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/1622-what-are-habitat-tokens-for/); [BlueStacks](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/dragon-city/drc-tips-tricks-en.html)). Storage caps grow steeply: the cheapest starter habitat holds **500 gold at level 1 and about 92,000 at level 7** ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Habitat/Upgrades)). The design also deliberately pairs fast earners with small-cap habitats and slow earners with large-cap ones ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/pages/beginners-guide)). Together these set the pace of return visits, and that pace lengthens as the player matures. An early habitat fills within minutes, which teaches the collect tap. Later caps stretch the return interval to hours, then overnight.

### Gold buys food, food buys levels, and level 10 bends the curve

Income grows linearly with level. A common single-element dragon starts at **7 gold per minute and adds 5 per level, and the increment halves after level 10** ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/dragon/Flame)). That gives the same 37 gold per minute at level 7 that a guide from about 2013 reported, so the formula has held for more than a decade ([Gamezebo, search snippet](https://www.gamezebo.com/?p=303255)). Higher rarities earn more ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/pages/beginners-guide)). Gold-boosting crystals add **20%**, and timed tower buffs add more ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/101-game-guides-1668600106/)).

Farms convert gold into food, and the crops build in a trade between being present and being efficient. In older data, the 30-second crop costs **5 gold per food**, while the 24- to 48-hour crops cost **50 gold per food** ([Gamelytic](https://gamelytic.com/dragon-city-complete-farm-and-food-guide/)). Crop and building XP roughly equal the gold spent, so player level in effect measures spending. Each dragon level takes four feedings, and the cost climbs in steep bands: **140 food to reach level 4, 1,280 to level 10, 21,820 to level 20, 565,660 to level 30, about 6.28 million to level 40, and about 113 million to level 70** ([Deetlist food costs](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/pages/food-costs)). A six-level building caps dragons at level 40, and empowerment stars raise the cap in steps to 70 ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/pages/food-costs); [Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4227-how-do-i-level-up-the-dragon-roost/)). The research notes estimate that levelling a common earner from 1 to 10 pays for itself in about 2.4 hours of extra income, while levelling from 10 to 20 takes about 68 hours. That gap splits the roster into economy dragons parked near level 10 and battle dragons pushed further for combat and level-gated breeding.

The island is the third set of gates. Player level runs to **200** and unlocks systems in stages: a mastery system at level 7, VIP access at 18 and a separate late-game world at 27 ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/101-game-guides-1668600106/)). Expansion plots cost gold or gems, but **new islands cost gems only** ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/625-how-do-i-expand-or-purchase-new-islands/)). The late-game world absorbs surplus gold, turning it into crystals for a closed collection of dragons that cannot fight ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_City)). Space therefore comes from three independent sources: habitats allowed by player level, tiles bought with gold or gems, and capacity upgraded with PvP tokens. No single currency removes every limit.

| Gate | How the genre applies it | What feeds it | Where premium currency cuts it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time | Breed, hatch, build and crop timers that scale with rarity and tier | Waiting | Gem skips, rewarded ads |
| Parallelism | Breeding sites and hatchery slots | Gems | Slot upgrades |
| Space | Habitat count by player level, island tiles | XP (roughly gold spent), gold | Gem-only islands |
| Capacity | Dragons per habitat, gold cap per habitat | Element tokens from PvP | Gem upgrades |
| Power | Banded food curve; level caps from a building and stars | Gold → food; dragon-specific orbs | Orb chests, offers |
| Odds | Parent rarity, empower, account-wide boost | Orbs, buildings | Empower |

## Shallow fights let collection and upgrades carry power

Combat is a turn-based **three-versus-three** duel. The faster dragon acts first, and the player picks one attack per turn ([BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips); [Help Center PvP](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/151-pvp-arenas/)). Damage depends mainly on a type multiplier of **2×, 1×, 0.5× or 0×**, read only against the defender's *primary* element. A dragon's other elements add no weaknesses; they only widen which attack types it can learn ([BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips); [Ditlep element chart](https://www.ditlep.com/information/elementchart)). The developer has published no damage formula, and the one fan reconstruction fits poorly ([Fandom forum, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/f/p/1897386997711135095)). Special skills such as first-hit immunity, life-steal and revive add some variety ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4182-what-are-special-skills/); [Help Center Perks](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/302-perks/)). Almost all the skill is in building the team before the fight; in battle, the main decision is picking the super-effective move. Two consequences follow. The 0× immunity is an absolute counter that forces players to collect many elements. And because only the primary element sets weaknesses while every element can attack, a hybrid is strictly better than a single-element dragon of the same tier, which gives players a built-in reason to breed up.

The type chart has also been the main source of power creep. A 2021 chart covered 14 elements; a 2026 guide counts **21** ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/dragon-city/element-weakness-chart/); [BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips)). Special elements tend to have fewer counters, and one class sits outside the chart entirely: it deals neutral damage to everything and has none of the usual weaknesses ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/dragon-city/element-weakness-chart/)).

Beyond levels, each dragon carries a stack of independent upgrade tracks, each paid in its own currency and fed by its own activity. **Empowerment stars** consume orbs of that exact dragon (or rarity-matched wildcard orbs), raise the level cap in steps from 45 to 70 and boost stats ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4184-how-can-i-empower-my-dragons/); [BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips)). **Ranks** add **+5% to +70%** HP and attack, with each step costing **5 to 500 defeated dragons** or rarity-matched coins sold in offers ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Battle?file=Combat-table-v2.png); [Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4669-where-can-i-find-rank-up-coins/)). **Perks** unlock at player level 14 and stack copies up to caps set by rarity; fan data puts a basic damage perk at about **+3% per copy, with caps of up to 30 copies** ([Help Center Perks](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/302-perks/); [Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Perks)). Innate or trained skills, set bonuses that buff every member of a skill family, and rarity tiers that keep extending upward complete the stack ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/101-game-guides-1668600106/)). Recalling an unwanted dragon returns orbs that scale with its level, so duplicates become upgrade material ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Tree_of_Life/Recall)). The research notes estimate that a fully upgraded top-rarity dragon has roughly **2–3× the stats** of the same dragon with no upgrades, the gap behind most pay-to-win complaints. Because every track has its own currency, an event can steer what players do simply by choosing which currency it pays out.

PvP is a trophy ladder that unlocks at player level 11. Wins earn trophies and losses can drop a player to a lower arena. Seasons last **7 days, with a partial trophy reset**, and each arena can restrict eligible dragons by element, rarity or level while boosting a featured element ([Help Center PvP](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/151-pvp-arenas/); [BlueStacks PvP, search snippet](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/dragon-city/drc-pvp-guide-en.html); [BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips)). The top three arenas reset every three weeks so that no one holds the prestige title permanently ([BlueStacks PvP](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/dragon-city/drc-pvp-guide-en.html)). A chest fills after **20 enemy knockouts** and pays orbs and habitat tokens ([Help Center PvP](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/151-pvp-arenas/)). A time-limited mode ranks players on small-group leaderboards instead of a global one ([official news](https://www.dragoncitygame.com/news/new-dragon-city-event-welcome-to-arena-rush)). Two details matter for a play-and-earn design. First, the store page advertises real-time arenas, but no source confirms whether opponents are live players or AI-controlled copies of player teams ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dragon-city-collect-battle/id561941526)). Second, the VIP program multiplies **arena leaderboard points** for higher spending tiers ([official news](https://www.dragoncitygame.com/news/new-feature-prestige-tiers)). That converts spending directly into rank, which would be indefensible where rank pays out cash.

## The event calendar, alliance chest and VIP cycle keep players paying attention

Dragon City's events run back to back and overlap. Its August 2026 calendar stacked a daily streak, a four-week VIP rerun, a six-week themed event, an 11- to 12-day race, two-week leaderboards and pairs of four- to seven-day mini-game islands, with **five to six events active at once** ([official calendar](https://www.dragoncitygame.com/news/upcoming-events-august-2026)). The mini-game islands (a maze, a fog-of-war grid, a tower climb, a runner, a puzzle) all reuse one idea with a new skin: earn a currency by playing the core loop, then spend it on a novel board to reach a featured dragon ([Deetlist events](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/events/)). The flagship race runs **11 days every three to four weeks** and always starts on a Thursday. It groups engaged players into small cohorts, which fan data puts at eight players ([Deetlist events](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/events/)). Anyone who reaches lap 15 is guaranteed the featured top-rarity dragon regardless of placement, and a per-period cap on points stops anyone finishing on day one ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/305-heroic-race/)). The same race is where spending matters most: gems speed up progress, and a small pack of event points sells for **$1.99** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dragon-city-collect-battle/id561941526)).

The social layer is light and cooperative. Alliances cap at **20 members** and share a chest that is always running. The chest rotates between four themes, each fed by an everyday action (arena battles, breeding, hatching, league battles) and paying a matching reward ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Alliances); [Deetlist news](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/news/article.php?id=15454)). Friendly alliance battles allow six fights in a row, energy refills after an hour, and a loss still pays 10% of a win's reward ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/101-game-guides-1668600106/); [BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips)). A competitive alliance race was reportedly cancelled after one event because of strongly negative feedback ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Alliances)). Trading is tightly controlled: players earn trade essences as rewards, and the top VIP tier gets one automatic trade a day ([official news](https://www.dragoncitygame.com/news/new-feature-prestige-tiers)). The lesson is that the guild layer works best when it adds value to play that already happens. Making strong and weak teammates race a deadline together is what players rejected.

Monetization combines the standard levers. **Gem packs** run **$1.99 to $19.99** with rotating discount variants of 20–30% ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dragon-city-collect-battle/id561941526)). A **$7.99 pass** has free and paid reward tracks fed by daily and weekly goals, and a second three-track pass sells orbs at fixed, non-random prices ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/306-divine-pass/)). Offers sell rank coins and perks, and gems skip timers ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4669-where-can-i-find-rank-up-coins/)). The VIP program runs on **21-day cycles and demotes players who miss a spending checkpoint** ([official news](https://www.dragoncitygame.com/news/new-feature-prestige-tiers)), which makes it a subscription in all but name, built on the fear of losing status. Card packs publish odds that are equal for every egg within a tier, from 11 eggs at 9.09% each to 30 eggs at 3.33% each, with no pity mechanism ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/2744-dragon-card-pack-probabilities/)), so chasing one specific egg in a 30-egg pool takes about 30 packs on average. Breeding odds, by contrast, are never published.

The complaints concentrate where money meets competition. A Finnish thesis comparing four games found that Dragon City used monetary dark patterns, such as pay-to-win, power creep and paying to skip waits, the most heavily of the four ([Theseus thesis, via search summary](https://www.theseus.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/921725/OConnor_Eleanor.pdf?sequence=2)). Forum players describe the race as "rigged pay-to-win" after spending tens of thousands of food on race tasks and winning nothing, and the developer keeps help articles about suspected hackers in races ([Fandom discussion, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/f/p/2785004417450270983/r/3496509656006943620); [Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/305-heroic-race/)). Parents cite heavy ads, pay-to-win pressure and unsafe chat ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/dragon-city/user-reviews/adult)). The developer's fixes are instructive because each one removes money or opacity from a competitive outcome: guaranteed race rewards at a progress threshold, small-group leaderboards, periodic resets in the top arenas, published pack odds, an optional confirmation before spending gems, and more dragons made breedable rather than sold only in offers ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/101-game-guides-1668600106/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dragon-city-collect-battle/id561941526)). Despite the complaints, the formula endures: **450 million downloads by 2022**, a **4.8-star rating from about 1.5 million ratings**, and more than **2,000 collectible dragons** in 2026 ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/dragon-city/ten-years-of-service-with-live-ops/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dragon-city-collect-battle/id561941526)). DragonVale, an older rival built on the same loop, reports more than 45 million downloads and more than $200 million in revenue ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DragonVale)).

## RaidLords puts only money on chain and caps every way out

RaidLords is a browser game about building a village and raiding others, and it follows the Clash of Clans structure closely ([RaidLords guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). It has a main building upgradable to level 12, three resources, troops, spells, heroes, three-minute raids scored in stars, trophy leagues, clans, clan wars and a shared clan village. On top of that it adds three things: a persistent knight that each player names (the Champion), a cap of **30 raids per day**, and bot-held "goblin" villages that fill matchmaking when few players are online. It runs in desktop and mobile browsers. Its developer is the pseudonymous 0xOziK, who previously built Stonewatch on the same chain, and the game opened on **October 26, 2026** ([EGamers.io, Oct 25](https://egamers.io/raidlords-gives-every-player-a-named-knight-called-the-champion-before-its-robinhood-chain-debut/); [EGamers.io, Oct 28](https://egamers.io/raidlords-burns-15-7m-tokens-as-4800-players-fight-75000-raids-in-robinhood-chain-debut/)). The game design itself is borrowed; the blockchain work is all in the economy layer.

| On chain (Robinhood Chain) | Off chain (server database) |
|---|---|
| One-time **$1 entry paid in ETH**, verified in about a minute | All buildings, troops, heroes, the Champion and its gear |
| RAID token transfers and purchases (launch curve or Uniswap v4) | Gold, essence, shadow, ores, clan currency, medals, books |
| Deposits into the vault (2% fee) | Every in-game purchase, priced in dollars and deducted from the deposited balance |
| Withdrawal claims using server-signed vouchers (5% fee, 100-token minimum) | Raids, matchmaking, trophies, clans, wars, seasons |
| Burns (transfers to the dead address) | Gold sales to the game for tokens, sell limits, bans |

Sources: [RaidLords guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/); [raidlords.com page source](https://raidlords.com/).

The token, **RAID**, is a plain ERC-20 with a **fixed supply of exactly 1 billion**. It has no owner, no mint function and no upgradeable proxy. The whole supply was minted in a single transaction through the Pons V2 meme-coin launchpad on launch day, with a 2.29 ETH initial buy ([on-chain reads via the public RPC](https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com); [Sourcify](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/4663/0xe33e9e479df8802cb0866d5d05258bec4cf62948); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/377349/pons-robinhood-chain-meme-coin-token-factory)). The game's own swap code buys on the launch curve until the token graduates at **4.2 ETH**, then buys through a Uniswap v4 pool ([swap.js](https://raidlords.com/game/swap.js)). A DexScreener snapshot on October 28 showed a price of about **$0.000231**, a fully diluted value of about **$216,000** and about **$43,000** of liquidity in the ETH pool ([DexScreener](https://dexscreener.com/robinhood/0xeba5c93b5adb45487d6daf9661632ba7e663011654dd9ccf561a1efbe490d656)). More than 15 copycat tokens using the RaidLords or RAID name exist on the chain, none linked to the official site ([DexScreener](https://api.dexscreener.com/latest/dex/search?q=RAID)).

Earning runs through a vault. A player deposits RAID, paying a 2% fee, and after that every in-game purchase is instant and free of gas. **Half of every token spent is burned and half goes to the reward vault** ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). The main way to earn is selling gold back to the game at a fixed **$0.08 per 1,000 gold**, paid in tokens at the day's price. Only gold from production and raids qualifies; gold bought with tokens or won in quests does not ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). The real control is a cap on how much gold a player may sell. New accounts may sell **$1 in their first 24 hours**; after that the daily limit is the higher of an allowance from a mint building (**$2 to $35 a day**, at a total cost of **$122** to fully upgrade) or the player's own deposits spread over two to five days. Once withdrawals exceed deposits the limit halves, above 2× deposits it drops to a fifth, and at 3× selling stops until the player spends more of their own deposited tokens ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). Filling even the top $35 allowance takes 437,500 qualifying gold a day, so production and the 30-raid cap, not the token, set the real ceiling on earnings. Withdrawals carry a 5% fee, and the vault enforces a **daily payout ceiling** that pushes excess claims to the next UTC day ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). Every other reward draws on the same vault: 14-day seasons that pay a vault share weighted by league, a weekly tournament with a $1 entry that pays 85% of the pot to the top fifth of players (the remaining 15% split between burn and vault), free leaderboards that pay every three days and daily, a 5% share of what referred friends spend, $0.50 per approved social-media post, and launch prize pools ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). A team post reportedly commits at least **0.5 ETH a day to buying RAID back into the vault**, but only a search snippet confirms it ([X, via search](https://x.com/raidlords)).

Most token sinks sell convenience or power rather than looks: extra builders ($0.30 to $6), protection shields ($0.15 to $0.38), speed-up potions, mint-building levels, a $0.75 season pass, a $0.75 clan loot boost and cosmetics, and above all the Champion, whose $1 unlock is followed by 20 valor ranks costing **$28.01** in total, paid arena runs and gear ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). The team reported **15.7 million tokens burned and 10.5 million withdrawn** in the first days ([EGamers.io](https://egamers.io/raidlords-burns-15-7m-tokens-as-4800-players-fight-75000-raids-in-robinhood-chain-debut/)). On-chain reads two days after launch found about **66.4 million RAID (6.6% of supply)** sitting at the dead address ([public RPC](https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com)). Spending always spikes at launch, so these numbers do not yet show the economy balances over time.

Fairness rests on rules the server enforces, not on on-chain proofs. The $1 entry gives every account a real cost. Leaderboard stars count only from raids on paid or bot-held villages, once per village per UTC day, which stops players farming their own spare accounts; referrals from the same internet connection earn nothing; matchmaking groups players by main-building level and trophies and counts Champion upgrades as trophies, so big spenders cannot hide in easy brackets; and raided players get 12–16 hours of protection ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). Bans confiscate everything earned in game, leaving a banned player able to withdraw only their own deposits. The team reported banning **7,779 bot and farm accounts**, more than its headline figure of 4,800+ players, and returning **2.6 million RAID** to the reward pool ([X, via search](https://x.com/raidlords); [EGamers.io](https://egamers.io/raidlords-burns-15-7m-tokens-as-4800-players-fight-75000-raids-in-robinhood-chain-debut/)). The same developer's earlier game published a wallet audit: 75 wallets flagged as bots were **$66,723 down** in total, while 2,361 player wallets were $18,569 up ([EGamers.io](https://egamers.io/raidlords-gives-every-player-a-named-knight-called-the-champion-before-its-robinhood-chain-debut/)).

The result looks more like capped earning backed by deposits than like classic play-to-earn. Ordinary players can take out a small fixed allowance or slowly recover their own deposits, and penalties start the moment they withdraw more than they put in. Two structural risks remain. The first is price: prices and payouts are set in dollars but paid in a floating token, so a falling price forces the vault to pay more tokens for the same dollar amount, a drain that feeds on itself and that the daily cap only slows. The second is reserve and custody. The vault holds about **498.6 million RAID, roughly half the supply** ([public RPC](https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com)); at the snapshot price that is about $115,000 of tokens against about $43,000 of pool liquidity (this report's calculation). The guide frames rewards as recycled spending, but in practice payouts draw just as much on a large reserve placed in the vault in advance and controlled by a single key, and heavy withdrawals would be sold into a thin market.

## A solo developer's stack shows how little blockchain a game needs

RaidLords' client is hand-written JavaScript with no framework, drawn on an HTML5 Canvas 2D context. It is one HTML file of roughly **685 KB** plus a few scripts, with no game engine and no build tooling ([page source](https://raidlords.com/)). Wallets connect through any wallet built into the browser, or on phones through Reown AppKit with the Wagmi adapter for WalletConnect. Email and social login are switched off, and deep links reopen the game inside the in-app browsers of the MetaMask, Coinbase, Trust and OKX wallets ([wallet.js](https://raidlords.com/game/wallet.js)). Sign-in is a one-time code plus a signed message that costs no gas. A comment in the code records that on launch day MetaMask would not show the sign-in message while the wallet was set to another chain, so the game must switch chains before asking for the signature ([page source](https://raidlords.com/)). A Telegram bot pushes alerts about raids, finished upgrades and prizes, and the guide is published in seven languages ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)).

The backend runs on Supabase, a hosted Postgres service, with roughly **200 database functions** that serve as the game's API ([config.js](https://raidlords.com/config.js)). Separate serverless functions handle wallet sign-in, battle checks, the vault, social-post checks and the Champion modes. The admin functions take parameters for the gold-to-dollar rate, a cap multiplier, a pause switch and the burn percentage, so the economy can be retuned while the game is live. Battles are checked by replaying them. The browser and the server share a simulator that always produces the same result from the same inputs: it uses whole-number coordinates, a fixed 30 ticks per second and a seeded random-number generator. The server re-runs the player's troop placements and rejects any result that does not match ([sim.js](https://raidlords.com/game/sim.js); [page source](https://raidlords.com/)).

On chain, RaidLords appears to own only two contracts: the token and a custom vault that pays out claims ([public RPC](https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com)). The researchers identified the vault from a function signature it shares with the game client and from its link to the RAID token; the team has not labelled it. It is **controlled by a single ordinary wallet rather than a multisig, can be paused, cannot be upgraded, and its source code is not verified** on Sourcify ([Sourcify](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/4663/0x05a3a6c50edb78ba65cb70ccc4b40f7426f0ecdf)). Withdrawals use cumulative vouchers. The server signs a running total of what a player is owed, and the contract pays the difference between that total and what the wallet has already claimed. The contract's error messages reveal a daily payout limit, voucher expiry, a withdrawal pause and holds on individual wallets ([page source](https://raidlords.com/)). Burns are done by sending tokens to the dead address rather than by calling the token's burn function ([public RPC](https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com)). The team switched from a model where players pre-approve the game to pull tokens from their wallet to this deposit vault on October 25, the day before launch ([page source](https://raidlords.com/)). It publishes no code repository, audit or terms of service ([GitHub search](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=raidlords); [guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)).

The chain supports a richer stack than RaidLords uses. Robinhood Chain is a permissionless Arbitrum-based layer-2 with gas paid in ETH, chain ID 4663 on mainnet and 46630 on testnet, and rate-limited public RPC endpoints meant for development, with Alchemy recommended for production ([docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain); [docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/connecting)). Gasless, walletless onboarding is fully supported: ERC-4337 EntryPoints v0.6–v0.8 and EIP-7702 are live, Alchemy Gas Manager and ZeroDev can pay players' gas, and Privy and Dynamic are named for embedded wallets ([docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/account-abstraction)). Randomness is the gap. Chainlink provides price feeds and CCIP, but **Chainlink VRF v2.5 does not support Robinhood Chain** ([docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/oracles-and-price-feeds); [Chainlink](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/supported-networks)), so breeding randomness must run on the server or use a commit-reveal scheme. Goldsky indexes the chain ([Goldsky](https://goldsky.com/blog/robinhood-chain-data-live-on-goldsky)), OpenSea has supported it since **July 11, 2026** ([OpenSea](https://opensea.io/blog/articles/robinhood-chain-is-live-on-opensea)), the Arbitrum canonical bridge, Relay, Across, LayerZero and CCIP all connect to it ([docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/bridging)), and Paxos USDG is a listed ecosystem partner that RAID already trades against ([docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain); [DexScreener](https://api.dexscreener.com/latest/dex/search?q=RaidLords)).

The chain's game scene is young, browser-first and built around tokens. Stonewatch, Play Kindra, Farm Valley, Hood Siege and Computers RH mostly pair a launchpad ERC-20 with burns on spending ([PlayToEarn](https://playtoearn.com/news/robinhood-chain-has-real-games-here-are-the-top-5-october-2026)). The research found **no dragon- or creature-breeding game on the chain**. Third-party reports put the chain's September 2026 NFT volume at about **$21.5 million** ([CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/robinhood-chain-nfts-stonkbrokers-erc-6551/)).

## Conclusion

Dragon City and RaidLords both ration something, but different things. Dragon City rations time and sells relief from it; RaidLords rations what players can take out and pays for it from what they spend. Wyrmhaven inherits both problems, because in a breeding game whose dragons can be minted and sold, every bought time-skip becomes a faster route to cash. The genre's most profitable levers become economically and legally toxic once a dragon has cash value: paid boosts to breeding odds, VIP multipliers on leaderboard points, and buyable event progress. Its fairness fixes become requirements: guaranteed rewards at a progress threshold, small-group leaderboards, caps per mode and published odds. The real design target is therefore not "Dragon City plus a token" but a version of the genre where money buys speed, space and looks, never rank or odds.

Ownership is both Wyrmhaven's differentiator and its biggest potential leak. RaidLords chose no NFTs and keeps full control of supply, and Dragon City restricts trading to essences it hands out. A tradable dragon turns the genre's emotional core, owning a creature you bred, into something liquid. It also lets money buy breeding stock and ranked power, and pushes paid breeding toward gambling. The workable middle path is to let NFTs carry collection value while ranked earning stays tied to skill and effort, through brackets, cooldowns and point sources that do not depend on luck. RaidLords also shows the team needs very little on-chain code. Two or three small contracts, with verified source and a multisig owner, would be a cheap trust advantage in an ecosystem where the leading game runs an unverified vault controlled by one key.

## Implications for the Wyrmhaven spec

This assessment reads the draft spec together with the economy and architecture documents it references, plus the game-core implementation plan. Where a figure is computed, it uses the spec's own formulas: gold per minute equals base gold times level, food to level up from L equals 4 × L², HP equals (100 + 20 × level) × stat multiplier, and Power equals (10 + 3 × level) × stat multiplier.

### What the draft gets right

| Draft choice | Why the research supports it |
|---|---|
| A pure, deterministic rules package (`game-core`) that runs on the server, with the clock and random-number generator passed in and a replay test | RaidLords needed a shared deterministic simulator plus a server replay to stop players submitting fake battle results ([sim.js](https://raidlords.com/game/sim.js)). Running every Wyrmhaven action on the server is stricter and keeps breeding results auditable. |
| Gameplay off chain; the chain used only for ownership and value | This is the standard pattern on the chain, and the reason RaidLords spending is instant and gas-free ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). |
| No on-chain randomness at MVP | Chainlink VRF v2.5 does not support Robinhood Chain ([Chainlink](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/supported-networks)), and the team's own chain notes already rule out randomness based on block hashes. |
| Rewards paid from a revenue pool, never more than the pool holds, claimed with Merkle proofs | This is the same principle as RaidLords' vault funded by spending, without the drain that comes from paying a floating token at a dollar price. In our view, one Merkle root per season is also easier to audit than a running voucher ledger. |
| No fungible token at launch | It avoids copycat tokens (15+ fake RAID tokens, [DexScreener](https://api.dexscreener.com/latest/dex/search?q=RAID)), exposure to the token's own price, and the securities risk the economy document flags. |
| A level-4 breeding gate that costs 56 food, against a starting stock of 50 | This matches the genre's habit of gates that teach rather than block: Dragon City's level-4 gate costs 140 food and is reached within minutes ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/pages/food-costs)). |
| Element-typed habitats where any of a dragon's elements qualifies | It keeps the genre's housing sink and settles a rule that Dragon City's FAQ leaves ambiguous ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4226-what-are-the-different-types-of-habitats/)). |
| A storage cap per habitat, and a guard so a clock moving backwards never removes gold | It keeps the core reason to come back (collect before the habitat overflows) and blocks manipulation of the device clock. |
| A minted dragon is locked in the game until it is returned | A dragon cannot be sold and played at the same time. RaidLords applies the same bookkeeping discipline to deposits and withdrawals. |
| Eight elements and no 0× immunity | It keeps the chart readable and avoids the absolute counters and "uncounterable" special classes that drove Dragon City's power creep ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/dragon-city/element-weakness-chart/)). |
| An audit and legal review before mainnet | RaidLords launched with no audit or terms of service that the researchers could find, and with an unverified vault ([GitHub search](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=raidlords); [Sourcify](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/4663/0x05a3a6c50edb78ba65cb70ccc4b40f7426f0ecdf)). This is a real point of difference. |

### What the draft is missing or gets wrong

The problems are ordered from most to least severe.

| Problem | Evidence | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The loop cannot start.** | The starting state has one dragon, breeding needs two different dragons, and the action set offers no way to get another: collect, buy food, feed, buy habitat, upgrade habitat, breed, hatch. The plan's breeding test creates two dragons by hand. | Give a second starter dragon of a different element, plus its habitat, or add a gold shop for commons. Dragon City sells a common for 100 gold ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/dragon/Flame)). |
| **2. The reward pool has no money inside MVP scope.** | The economy document funds the pool from marketplace fees and gem and pass sales. The spec excludes the gem store and defines no pass. Marketplace fees are optional for players, because a standard ERC-721 can be transferred directly or sold on OpenSea, which already supports the chain ([OpenSea](https://opensea.io/blog/articles/robinhood-chain-is-live-on-opensea)). The pool formula leaves out the mint fee, the one fee the game fully controls. | Fund the pool from fees on steps every earner must go through: an earning licence or entry fee, the mint fee, a fee for returning a dragon to the game, and a season pass. RaidLords does the same with its $1 entry and its 2% deposit and 5% withdrawal fees ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). |
| **3. Breeding punishes rare parents.** | A child can be any species whose elements all appear in the parents' combined elements. Take two pure commons whose element pair also has a rare and an epic: they produce the epic 9/159 ≈ **5.7%** of the time. Two rare hybrids covering four elements, even with no other rares in that pool, produce the same epic 9/309 ≈ **2.9%** of the time and a common **78%** of the time. Every extra rare that fits the four elements makes this worse. The best strategy is never to breed rares, the opposite of the genre, where rarer parents raise the odds of top tiers ([Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/4221-how-can-i-boost-my-breeding-chances/)). | Pick one element from each parent and draw from that pair's pool. Give rarer parents a bonus toward the top tiers. Reserve epics and the legendary for specific parent-pair recipes gated by level. |
| **4. Habitat caps reverse the usual pacing of return visits.** | One level-1 common (2 gold a minute) takes **500 minutes** to fill a level-1 habitat. Three level-20 legendaries (1,800 gold a minute) fill a fully upgraded 5,000-gold habitat in **under 3 minutes**. Capacity stays at 3 dragons, and new habitats cost a flat 500 gold with no limit on how many. Dragon City's caps grow about 180-fold across levels, and its habitat count is limited by player level ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Habitat/Upgrades); [Help Center](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/faq/632-how-many-habitats-can-i-build/)). | Define each cap as hours of that habitat's current production: minutes to an hour early on, rising toward overnight at the top level. Let capacity grow with upgrades, and raise the price of each additional habitat of the same element. |
| **5. Combat is an underspecified stat check.** | There is no team size, turn order, choice of attack, defense stat or randomness. Example: a level-20 common attacking at 2× and a level-20 legendary attacking back at 0.5× each need exactly 8 hits, so whoever moves first wins, and the spec does not say who that is. A dragon's second element has no use in combat. In asynchronous PvP, the attacker can always bring the counter element. | Adopt the genre baseline: 3v3, a speed stat that sets turn order, a choice each turn of attacking with the primary or secondary element, weakness set by the primary element only, and a published damage formula ([BoostRoom](https://boostroom.com/blog/dragon-city-battle-guide-teams-elements-and-combat-tips)). |
| **6. The first session never produces a dragon.** | A common takes **30 minutes**, where the genre uses **30 seconds** to breed and 30 to hatch ([Deetlist](https://deetlist.com/dragoncity/dragon/Flame)). Starter income of 2 gold a minute, against 1,000 starting gold, makes the first collect tap meaningless. | Make the tutorial breed finish in under a minute, keep common timers in minutes, and tune starter income so the first habitat fills during the first session. |
| **7. Duplicates and eggs clog the single nest.** | Two pure commons return a common 75–80% of the time, depending on which rarer species share their pair. There is no way to sell or release a dragon. An egg with no free matching slot stays in the nest and blocks breeding. | Let players release dragons for an upgrade material, as Dragon City's recall turns dragons into upgrade orbs ([Fandom, search snippet](https://dragoncity.fandom.com/wiki/Tree_of_Life/Recall)). Add a small egg inventory and a gold sale price. |
| **8. Gold has nothing to spend on in the late game.** | Every sink is finite: about **19,760 gold** of food takes any dragon from level 1 to 20, and **10,500 gold** buys and fully upgrades a habitat. The "breeding fees" listed in the economy document are missing from the spec. | Add a breeding fee that rises with parent rarity, rising habitat prices and gold-priced cosmetics. |
| **9. Season Points for "rare breeds" pay cash for luck.** | The economy document itself flags paying real value for random outcomes as a gambling risk. Dragon City does not even publish its breeding odds. | Award points for milestones that do not depend on luck, such as the first time a player discovers each species. Show the odds for each pairing in the interface, and guarantee a rare result after a set number of misses. |
| **10. The type chart contradicts itself.** | The table lists gale as strong against frost and frost as strong against gale, so each deals 2× to the other. Yet the spec names only umbra and lumen as a mutual pair. As a result, gale and frost resist one element each instead of two, and no element resists umbra or lumen. | Confirm what was intended. If it is a mistake, change one row and re-run the balance checks. |
| **11. Nothing stops collusion or farming wins in volume.** | The economy document caps daily points and requires a minimum account age, but sets no rules on who counts as a valid opponent. RaidLords counts leaderboard stars only against paid or bot-held villages, once per village per day, caps raids at 30 a day, and pays nothing for referrals from the same internet connection ([guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). | Adopt equivalent rules, and publish terms that allow confiscating earnings from banned accounts. |
| **12. Seasons are undefined.** | Season length, reset rule and payout curve are not specified. Dragon City uses 7-day seasons with partial resets and guaranteed rewards at progress thresholds. RaidLords uses 14-day seasons with payouts weighted by league ([Help Center PvP](https://socialpoint.helpshift.com/hc/en/7-dragon-city/section/151-pvp-arenas/); [guide](https://raidlords.com/guide/)). | Specify the length, the reset rule and how rewards split between thresholds and placement (see decisions 4 and 13 below). |

### Open design decisions

| Decision | Recommendation | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Fungible token or not** | Launch without a token, and pay seasons in USDG or ETH. Paxos USDG is a listed partner of the chain ([docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain)). | Gives up the launch buzz and trading activity a token brings (RaidLords reported 4,800+ players within days). In return it avoids copycat tokens, the drain from dollar-priced payouts, and securities exposure. |
| **2. How the pool is funded** | Publish a fixed share of every unavoidable fee (earning licence, minting, returning a dragon to the game, season pass) that goes to the pool, with a hard cap per season. | Early pools will be small and earnings modest, which weakens "earn" marketing. In return the pool can never pay out more than it takes in. |
| **3. Who may earn** | Let anyone play the full loop free as a guest. To earn, require a one-time paid earning licence of about $1, a linked wallet, an account at least 7 days old and minimum progress. | The extra step shrinks the number of players who earn. Without a cost per account, expect bot pressure on RaidLords' scale (7,779 bans). |
| **4. What earns Season Points** | Award points for PvP wins against eligible opponents (each opponent counts once per day), quests and first-time species discoveries, with a daily cap. Pay most of the pool through progress thresholds and a minority through capped placement within small groups. | Less jackpot excitement. In return it avoids paying cash for luck and leaderboards dominated by big spenders, the source of Dragon City's "rigged" complaints. |
| **5. NFT scope** | Allow minting only for dragons of rare rarity or above, for a fee. Make returned NFTs wait out a cooldown before entering ranked play, and cap level or rarity in ranked brackets. | Less "own everything" appeal. In return the marketplace cannot sell season rank, and it is not flooded with commons. |
| **6. Custom marketplace or OpenSea** | Launch on OpenSea and postpone the custom Marketplace contract. Collect revenue at minting and when dragons return to the game. | No guaranteed fee on trades, but one fewer contract to audit and ship. |
| **7. Breeding model and disclosure** | Draw from element-pair pools, give rarer parents a bonus, gate the top tiers behind specific parent-pair recipes at a set level, show the odds for each pairing, and guarantee a rare result after a set number of misses. | More data and interface work, and less mystery. Required anyway once bred dragons can be sold. |
| **8. Proving the randomness is fair** | Keep the server-generated seed (a secret plus the action ID), but publish a cryptographic hash of each season's secret before the season and reveal the secret afterwards, so players can check results. | A small engineering cost, and it exposes the game's tuning to players who optimise hard. It gives verifiable fairness without needing VRF. |
| **9. Combat and PvP format** | Asynchronous 3v3 battles against saved defending teams, turn order by speed, a choice of attacking element each turn, matchmaking by rating band, and 20–30 attacks a day. | More scope for roadmap phase 5. Real-time PvP stays out of scope. |
| **10. What to sell in the MVP** | Sell time and capacity (extra nests, hatch skips), cosmetics and a season pass. Never sell food, gold, levels, Season Points or leaderboard multipliers. | Paying for speed still helps payers collect faster, so keep speed purchases out of anything ranked. |
| **11. Wallets and onboarding** | Start players as guests. Create an embedded wallet (Privy or Dynamic) the first time they act to earn, and pay their gas for minting and claiming through Alchemy Gas Manager or ZeroDev. Also support browser wallets and WalletConnect, with deep links into wallets' in-app browsers. | Cost of, and dependence on, an outside provider. In return far less wallet hassle than RaidLords' requirement to hold ETH before playing. |
| **12. Contract governance** | Use a Safe multisig as owner, verify the source code on Blockscout and Sourcify, build a pause switch and a per-season cap into the RewardDistributor, and publish the official contract addresses at launch. | Slower response in an emergency. It closes the trust gap left by RaidLords' unverified vault controlled by one key. |
| **13. Season length and content** | Run 14-day paid seasons with weekly milestones and one featured species per season, earned by reaching a threshold rather than by placement. | Each season costs new content. Longer seasons also mean fewer Merkle roots and fewer claim transactions than weekly payouts. |
| **14. Bringing players back** | Add Telegram or web push alerts for ready eggs and full habitats from day one. | One more integration. Timers that reward coming back only work if players are reminded, which is why RaidLords built a Telegram bot. |
